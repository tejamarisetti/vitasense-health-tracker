  "abstract": "<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>\n                  <jats:p>High false alarm rates in the ICU decrease quality of care by slowing staff response times while increasing patient delirium through noise pollution. The 2015 PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenge provides a set of 1250 multi-parameter ICU data segments associated with critical arrhythmia alarms, and challenges the general research community to address the issue of false alarm suppression using all available signals. Each data segment was 5\u2009minutes long (for real time analysis), ending at the time of the alarm. For retrospective analysis, we provided a further 30\u2009seconds of data after the alarm was triggered.</jats:p>\n                  <jats:p>A total of 750 data segments were made available for training and 500 were held back for testing. Each alarm was reviewed by expert annotators, at least two of whom agreed that the alarm was either true or false. Challenge participants were invited to submit a complete, working algorithm to distinguish true from false alarms, and received a score based on their program\u2019s performance on the hidden test set. This score was based on the percentage of alarms correct, but with a penalty that weights the suppression of true alarms five times more heavily than acceptance of false alarms.</jats:p>\n                  <jats:p>We provided three example entries based on well-known, open source signal processing algorithms, to serve as a basis for comparison and as a starting point for participants to develop their own code. A total of 38 teams submitted a total of 215 entries in this year\u2019s Challenge.</jats:p>\n                  <jats:p>\n                    This editorial reviews the background issues for this challenge, the design of the challenge itself, the key achievements, and the follow-up research generated as a result of the Challenge, published in the concurrent special issue of\n                    <jats:italic>Physiological Measurement</jats:italic>\n                    . Additionally we make some recommendations for future changes in the field of patient monitoring as a result of the Challenge.\n                  </jats:p>",
